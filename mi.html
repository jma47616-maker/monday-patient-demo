        Teaching pearls
        • The most dangerous ECG is the one filed as normal. Biphasic or deeply inverted T-waves in V2–V3 in a pain-free patient with a quiet troponin are Wellens syndrome — a critical proximal LAD announcing itself. No treadmill test, ever — it can precipitate the infarct. Angiography.
        • Myocardial infarction does not owe you chest pain. He never reported any — sweating, vomiting and a sense of dread were the whole story. The "never-ill" middle-aged smoker is exactly who presents this way.
        • Troponin is a trend, not a number. 148 alone could be demand from any tachycardia; 148 → 892 with evolving anterior changes is a type 1 infarct (Fourth Universal Definition). And never let a raised CRP talk you out of a rising troponin.
        • A murmur you can hear, splinters in the nailbeds and a tender nodule on a finger pulp make a compelling endocarditis picture — and it earns three culture sets before any antibiotic (ESC 2023) and an echo. But a murmur is a finding, not a diagnosis: no vegetation, nothing growing, and a joiner's hands that take a battering every day. What none of it explains is Saturday's tracing, or a troponin going 148 → 892.
        • Every decoy explained part of the picture — the workshop bug the diarrhoea, the trade the splinters, the extraction the endocarditis story. Only the LAD explains Saturday's tracing, the troponin trend and the collapse together.
        • Cardiogenic hypotension is not an empty tank. The reflex fluid bolus floods a failing ventricle, and the reflex non-rebreathe harms too — in ACS, oxygen only below SpO₂ 90% (AVOID, DETO2X-AMI; ESC 2023).
        • Watch the QT you already own. Sertraline had his QTc at 476 ms before you prescribed anything — ondansetron on top invites torsades. Metoclopramide spares the QT.
