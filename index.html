          Teaching pearls
          • Dark stool blamed on iron is the classic decoy. Iron stains; melaena is black, tarry, sticky and offensive. When in doubt, do the PR and let the urea settle it.
          • Urea 14.6 with a normal creatinine — out of proportion — is the fingerprint of an upper GI bleed. Digested blood is a protein meal.
          • The troponin of 34 is a type-2 rise from demand on a haemoglobin of 72. Treat it as ACS and you anticoagulate a bleeding man.
          • Glasgow-Blatchford risk-stratifies before endoscopy. A 0 can go home; this man, with a high score, cannot.
          • Terlipressin saves lives in variceal bleeding — but it's a vasoconstrictor. His cardiac history isn't just a diagnostic decoy; it shapes whether you reach for it.
