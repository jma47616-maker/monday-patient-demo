    Teaching — the 2025 essentials
    • In a refractory arrest the rhythm is a symptom, and there can be more than one cause. Here there were two — hyperkalaemia from missed dialysis and septic shock from the line — and fixing only one keeps him arrested. Work the whole 4 Hs and 4 Ts.
    • Calcium (gluconate or chloride) is the membrane-stabilising priority in a hyperkalaemic arrest — give it before insulin–dextrose shifts potassium.
    • Adrenaline 1 mg: straight away in non-shockable rhythms, but only after the third shock in VF/pVT — then every 3–5 minutes. Amiodarone 300 mg after three shocks, 150 mg after five.
    • 2025 emphases: IV access first (IO only after two failed attempts), highest feasible oxygen with ventilation at 10/min once the airway is secured, and waveform capnography — a sudden EtCO₂ rise is often your first sign of ROSC.
